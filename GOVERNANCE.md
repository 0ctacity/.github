# Octacity Governance

## Purpose

This document defines how Octacity is organized, how authority is delegated, and how decisions are made across the community.

Octacity is an open-source community structured around eight main teams. The organization is guided by an Organization Lead, while team leaders manage their teams and maintaining teams direct their repositories.

The governance model is designed to give teams broad independence without sacrificing organizational accountability, long-term reliability, or the health of the wider community.

## The Organization Lead

Octacity is led by an Organization Lead.

The founder serves as the first Organization Lead and holds the highest authority within the organization. The Organization Lead is responsible for managing the organization, coordinating its team leaders, protecting its long-term direction, and taking action when necessary to preserve the community.

The Organization Lead has final authority over:

- Organization membership
- Appointment and removal of team leaders
- Organization-wide structure and policy
- Repository and project intervention
- Disputes that cannot be resolved within or between teams
- Changes to this governance document
- Any matter that may materially affect Octacity's safety, legality, integrity, or long-term health

The Organization Lead may reject, reverse, suspend, or replace decisions made elsewhere in the organization.

This authority exists as a safeguard, not as a requirement for routine involvement. Octacity is intended to operate with as little unnecessary micromanagement as possible. Team leaders and maintaining teams are expected to exercise their own judgment and manage their responsibilities independently.

The Organization Lead intervenes when necessary, particularly where there is:

- Serious organizational risk
- Legal or security concern
- Misconduct
- Persistent inactivity
- Loss of trust
- Conflict between teams
- Failure of project maintenance
- Harm to Octacity's long-term direction or reliability

The ability to intervene does not imply an intention to control every decision.

## Organization Lead Succession

The current Organization Lead may appoint the next Organization Lead.

No broader succession mechanism is defined at this stage of Octacity's development.

## The Eight Main Teams

Octacity is represented by eight main teams.

Each team:

- Has its own team leader
- May contain any number of members, subject to Octacity's membership requirements
- May organize its work according to its own needs
- May create and maintain repositories
- May establish subteams for projects or specialized responsibilities
- May collaborate with any other Octacity team

Subteams do not replace the eight-team structure. They exist within or beneath a main team and support its work.

A team leader may create subteams when useful. The Organization Lead retains the authority to object to, reorganize, or remove a subteam when necessary.

## Team Leaders

Team leaders are appointed and may be removed or replaced by the Organization Lead.

A team leader has broad authority within their team. They are responsible for its organization, members, projects, repository access, internal coordination, and continued activity.

A team leader may:

- Organize the team's work
- Assign or delegate responsibilities
- Manage team membership
- Create subteams
- Approve new project proposals
- Determine how repositories are maintained
- Assign maintainers and reviewers
- Decide how contributions are reviewed
- Remove access to repositories maintained by the team
- Resolve internal disagreements
- Coordinate collaboration with other teams
- Transfer project maintenance when another team agrees to take responsibility

A team leader may delegate responsibilities to members of their team or qualified members of another team.

Team leaders are expected to act in the interests of their team, its projects, and Octacity as a whole.

The Organization Lead may invalidate or reverse a team leader's authority or decisions when necessary.

## Team Members

Members belong to one of Octacity's main teams but are encouraged to contribute throughout the organization.

Membership in one team does not restrict a person from:

- Contributing to another team's repositories
- Reviewing work outside their team
- Participating in cross-team projects
- Sharing technical knowledge
- Helping resolve issues across the organization

Cross-team contribution is strongly encouraged.

Members are expected to:

- Participate consistently
- Communicate clearly
- Follow through on agreed responsibilities
- Respect project and repository rules
- Keep their team informed about meaningful changes in availability
- Contribute honestly and constructively
- Follow the Octacity Code of Conduct

## Membership Admission

Membership follows this process:

1. Application
2. Review with team leaders
3. Interview
4. Final decision

Team leaders participate in reviewing applicants and determining appropriate team placement.

The Organization Lead makes the final decision on admission to the Octacity organization.

Applicants do not select their team as part of the application. Team placement is determined during review according to the applicant's interests, experience, potential fit, and the current needs of Octacity's teams.

## Team Placement and Transfers

Team leaders determine the most suitable placement for accepted members.

A member may later move between teams when the relevant team leaders agree. The Organization Lead must be notified of the transfer.

Members remain free to contribute across teams regardless of their primary placement.

## Membership Removal

Repository access may be removed by the leader of the maintaining team.

Team leaders may arrange a member's removal from or transfer between teams. Transfers should be coordinated with the receiving team leader and communicated to the Organization Lead.

Removal from the Octacity organization is decided by the Organization Lead.

Membership may be reconsidered or ended because of:

- Persistent inactivity
- Repeated failure to communicate
- Misconduct
- Loss of trust
- Repeated disregard for repository or community rules
- Harmful or disruptive behavior
- Misrepresentation
- Failure to meet agreed responsibilities
- Other conduct that threatens the community's health or reliability

## Participation and Inactivity

Active participation is a core expectation of Octacity membership.

Activity is not measured only by code or raw hours. Meaningful participation may include:

- Completing agreed contributions
- Making substantive progress
- Reviewing work
- Researching project decisions
- Writing documentation
- Participating in technical discussions
- Coordinating work
- Reporting blockers clearly
- Supporting other contributors

The activity of members may be reviewed by the Organization Lead together with their team leaders.

Inactivity is taken seriously. A member may be removed when they remain inactive, fail to communicate, or repeatedly do not meet their agreed level of participation.

Temporary inactivity is not automatically a violation. Members should communicate periods of reduced availability, including examinations, work commitments, personal circumstances, or other reasonable limitations.

Communication is essential. Unexplained inactivity is treated differently from an openly communicated absence.

## Leaving and Returning

Members may leave Octacity voluntarily.

They are expected to communicate their decision and provide enough notice or context for their responsibilities to be transferred safely.

Former members may later apply to return. Previous participation, departure circumstances, and communication will be considered.

Repeated cycles of joining and leaving may result in a future application being declined, particularly when they create instability for teams or projects.

## Maintaining Teams

Every Octacity repository has a maintaining team.

The team that creates a repository becomes its maintaining team by default.

The maintaining team is responsible for:

- Project direction
- Scope and roadmap
- Architecture
- Issue prioritization
- Pull request review
- Releases
- Documentation
- Repository access
- Security and maintenance
- Long-term project sustainability

The maintaining team has final authority over ordinary repository and project decisions.

Members of other teams and public contributors may participate, but the maintaining team retains responsibility for reviewing and directing the work.

## Repository Review Authority

The maintaining team's leader determines how repository decisions and contributions are reviewed.

They may:

- Review work directly
- Delegate review to a team member
- Appoint repository maintainers
- Request review from another team
- Assign responsibility according to expertise

Repository-specific rules may define additional review requirements.

## Project Creation

A team leader may approve the creation of a new Octacity project or repository.

The Organization Lead retains the authority to reject or reverse that approval.

New projects should be:

- Realistic
- Useful
- Suitable for long-term development
- Contributable by multiple people
- Maintainable by the responsible team
- Beneficial to the community or the wider open-source ecosystem
- Distinct enough to justify their existence

Teams should inspect existing Octacity projects before proposing new ones and avoid unnecessarily reinventing work that already exists.

Projects should not be created only for temporary excitement, a short demonstration, or an idea likely to be abandoned after initial implementation.

Before creating a project, the proposing team should consider:

- Who will maintain it
- How contributors can participate
- Whether its scope is sustainable
- Whether it duplicates another project
- What long-term value it provides
- How it will remain active after its initial release

Octacity should not become an archive of abandoned experiments.

## Project Transfer and Retirement

A maintaining team may transfer a project to another team when both teams agree.

The Organization Lead may intervene in a transfer when necessary.

When a maintaining team no longer wishes or is no longer able to continue a project, it should first seek another team willing to assume responsibility.

If no suitable maintaining team can be found, the project should be archived.

Repositories should not remain presented as active when no team is prepared to maintain them.

## Cross-Team Collaboration

Teams are encouraged to contribute to one another's projects.

Cross-team collaboration may include:

- Code contributions
- Reviews
- Architecture discussions
- Research
- Documentation
- Design
- Infrastructure
- Security work
- Testing
- Project coordination

Responsibility follows relevance and expertise, but the maintaining team retains final authority over its repository.

When responsibilities overlap, teams should communicate openly and determine which team is best positioned to lead the work.

## Internal Disagreements

Disagreements within a team should first be discussed openly and constructively.

Members should explain their reasoning, provide relevant evidence, and attempt to reach a workable conclusion.

The team leader makes the decision when consensus cannot be reached.

When the team leader is directly involved in a serious disagreement, or the dispute becomes harmful or unmanageable, the Organization Lead may enter the discussion and make the final decision.

## Disagreements Between Teams

Teams should communicate directly when work, responsibilities, or project scopes overlap.

The maintaining team has final authority over decisions concerning its repository.

Teams should avoid duplicating existing Octacity projects without a clear reason and should examine whether collaboration or extension is more appropriate than creating a competing project.

The Organization Lead resolves conflicts that cannot be settled between team leaders.

## Legal and Security Responsibility

Each maintaining team is responsible for managing its repositories and work lawfully and responsibly.

This includes reasonable attention to:

- Licensing
- Intellectual property
- Privacy
- Security
- Data handling
- Dependencies
- External services
- Distribution
- Applicable legal restrictions

Repository decisions can create serious or irreversible consequences. Teams must not treat legal or security concerns casually.

The Organization Lead may immediately intervene when a project, repository, release, or team action creates credible legal, security, or organizational risk.

## Governance Changes

This document may only be changed with the approval of the Organization Lead.

The Organization Lead may consult team leaders or other members before making a change but retains final authority over the governance of Octacity.

## Guiding Principle

Octacity combines team independence with organizational accountability.

Teams should be trusted to lead their work, members should be encouraged to contribute across the community, and intervention should remain limited to situations where it is genuinely needed.

The Organization Lead's authority exists to protect Octacity's health, direction, and long-term reliability—not to replace the judgment and initiative of its teams.
